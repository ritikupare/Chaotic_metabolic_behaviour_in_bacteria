This page solves the four chemostat equations live, hour by hour, right here in your browser — the same model documented in the repository, reproduced from Molz, Faybishenko & Agarwal (2019). Watch nutrient, rods, cocci and predators rise and fall together, then watch two almost-identical starting points drift apart below — that drift is what deterministic chaos looks like, and the point where it becomes unmistakable is the Lyapunov time.

Trajectory B starts one part in ten million away from A in nutrient concentration — a difference no lab instrument could ever measure. For a long while the two look identical. Then, without anything changing, they peel apart. τ marks one Lyapunov time (≈ 846 hr, ≈ 35 days) — the offline-computed timescale over which any forecast of this system, however good, decays into guesswork.

Phase portrait — rods vs cocci colour = predator density

Every point is one instant in time. In the chaotic regime these points never repeat, yet they never leave a bounded, folded shape — the strange attractor. Switch to a die-out regime and watch it collapse to a line or a point.

dn/dt  = D(n0−n) − (μrn/Yrn)(n/(Krn+n))mrr − (μcn/Ycn)(n/(Kcn+n))mcc + EF·δp·mpp
dr/dt  = μrn(n/(Krn+n))r − (μpr(m₁r+i₁)/Ypr)(mrr/(Kpr+m₂r+mrr))(mpp/mr) − Dr
dc/dt  = μcn(n/(Kcn+n))c − (μpc/Ypc)(mcc/(Kpc+mcc))(mpp/mc) − Dc
dp/dt  = μpr(m₁r+i₁)(mrr/(Kpr+m₂r+mrr))p + μpc(mcc/(Kpc+mcc))p − Dp − δpp

Parameters are Table 1.1 of the LBNL report. This page uses a fixed-step RK4 solver tuned for smooth animation; the repository's authoritative Lyapunov exponent, 0–1 test and bifurcation diagram are computed separately in Python with an adaptive-step solver — see README.md and src/chemostat.py.
